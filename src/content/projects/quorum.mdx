## Background

Quorum is an incremental systems design game for software engineers. You found a startup with one virtual machine in a garage. Users send requests, you wire components on a board, and you fix one bottleneck after another until the company serves billions of people and goes public.

Every mechanic comes from real system behaviour. PHP-FPM workers wait on the database, a Node.js event loop stalls on bcrypt, a JVM is slow until it warms up, and an average hides the p99. If you have been paged at 3 AM, you know the problems. The game asks you to fix them with numbers.

## How it plays

- The simulation is a flow network. One node limits the most revenue at any time, and the board marks it and says why: CPU, credits, RAM, workers, or connections.
- Each fix moves the bottleneck. A cache removes database load and adds stale reads. A read replica adds capacity and adds replication lag.
- Each app server runs a stack, a runtime and a framework. No stack strictly beats another.
- There are no accounts. A company lives in your browser, and you can copy it out as text to back it up.

The [Quorum Wiki](https://wiki.playquorum.dev) is the manual and a free systems design reference.
